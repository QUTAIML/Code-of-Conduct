# QUT AI & ML Society Code of Conduct
The QUT AI & ML Society is committed to creating a welcoming, respectful, and inclusive community for all members. We are dedicated to fostering an environment where everyone, regardless of background or experience, can collaborate, learn, and grow.
By participating in our society, you agree to abide by the following Code of Conduct.
Community members who violate these standards may be subject to disciplinary action, including warnings, temporary removal, or permanent expulsion from the community, at the discretion of the Executive Committee.
<br/>

---

<br/>

# Expected Standards of Conduct

All members, volunteers, executive members, speakers, sponsors, mentors, and attendees are expected to:
- Treat all participants with respect, courtesy, and professionalism.
- Foster a welcoming, inclusive, and collaborative environment.
- Respect differing viewpoints and experiences.
- Follow reasonable directions provided by Executive Committee members, event volunteers, venue staff, or security.
- Act honestly and with integrity when representing the Society.
- Respect the privacy and confidentiality of members where appropriate.
- Help create an environment where everyone feels safe to participate, learn, and contribute.
- Obtain explicit consent before any physical contact with another person, or before using another person's belongings or equipment.
- Recognise that disagreement with a decision, direction, or another person's opinion is never a valid excuse for poor behaviour, rudeness, or hostility.
- Recognise that freedom of speech does not mean freedom from consequence. While the Society supports free expression and tries to avoid unnecessary censorship, conduct that is harmful or inconsistent with this Code may still result in a warning, removal from an event, or other disciplinary action.
- Comply with all reasonable and lawful instructions given by organisers, whether or not the member personally agrees with the direction given, including instructions about the fair and appropriate use of shared or limited event resources such as tools, equipment, or printing services.

# Harassment Free Environment

We do not tolerate harassment or discrimination in any form. Harassment includes, but is not limited to:
- Offensive comments related to:
  - Gender (including identity, history, or expression)
  - Sexual orientation
  - Disability
  - Physical appearance
  - Race or ethnicity
  - Religion
  - Academic background or programming experience
- Sexual images, language, or jokes in public or shared spaces
- Deliberate intimidation, stalking, or following
- Harassing photography or recording
- Sustained disruption of talks, events, or online discussions
- Unwelcome physical contact or sexual attention
- Threats or incitement to violence or self harm
- Breaches of digital privacy or sharing personal information without consent
- Emotionally manipulative or degrading behaviour directed at another participant, even where no single incident would independently amount to harassment
- Repeated unwanted interactions with a person who has indicated, directly or through their behaviour, that the interaction is unwelcome
All members are expected to comply with requests to stop any harassing behaviour immediately.

# Enforcement
The Executive Committee is responsible for enforcing this Code of Conduct. Where a breach is identified, the Executive Committee will consider the nature, severity, impact, and any previous conduct when determining an appropriate outcome. The Executive Committee will also consider the overall body of information available to it, including direct observations by organisers, contemporaneous reports, and accounts provided by affected or witnessing participants, rather than relying on a single report in isolation.

Possible actions include, but are not limited to:

### 1. Formal Warning
A formal warning may be issued where a member's conduct falls below the expectations of this Code of Conduct.
A formal warning will generally:
- Describe the conduct that gave rise to the warning.
- Identify the relevant sections of the Code of Conduct that have been breached.
- Outline the expected standard of conduct moving forward.
- Advise that further breaches may result in more serious disciplinary action.
Formal warnings may be issued verbally or in writing, however the Executive Committee may elect to provide written confirmation for record keeping purposes.

### 2. Interim Removal or Suspension at an Event
Where the Executive Committee reasonably believes that a participant's conduct presents a risk to the safety or wellbeing of others at an event, the Executive Committee may remove or suspend that participant from the event immediately, without first completing a full investigation.

An interim removal or suspension:
- Is an event management decision, made to protect the safety and wellbeing of participants and organisers, and is not by itself a finding that any particular allegation has been established.
- May be based on a combination of direct observations by organisers, contemporaneous reports from participants, and the participant's own conduct towards organisers while the matter is being managed.
- Does not prevent the participant from later raising a complaint, seeking a review of the decision, or providing their own account of events through an appropriate process.
- Does not replace, and is separate from, any formal investigation or disciplinary process that may follow, whether run by the Society, the University, or another appropriate body.
A member who is removed or suspended under this provision will, where reasonably practicable, be told in general terms why the decision has been made, without requiring the Executive Committee to disclose the identity of individuals who provided information in confidence.

### 3. Removal from the Executive Team
Executive members are expected to uphold a higher standard of professionalism and leadership.
An Executive Committee member may be removed from their position where they:
- Repeatedly breach this Code of Conduct;
- Fail to comply with a formal warning;
- Engage in conduct that significantly damages the reputation, operation, or integrity of the Society;
- Engage in harassment, discrimination, intimidation, bullying, or other serious misconduct; or
- Otherwise fail to meet the responsibilities expected of an executive member.
Removal from an executive position may occur immediately where the Executive Committee considers the breach sufficiently serious.
This may include:
- Permanent removal from Society managed online communities;
- Prohibition from attending Society events;
- Removal from Society projects, committees, or volunteer roles; and
- Any other reasonable action necessary to protect the safety, wellbeing, and operation of the Society.

### 4. Referral to QUT or Other Authorities

Where conduct may breach University policies, applicable laws, or create a risk to the safety or wellbeing of others, the Executive Committee may refer the matter to the Queensland University of Technology, the QUT Guild, venue management, security personnel, or law enforcement where appropriate. Where the conduct involves behaviour of the kind described in the QUT Student Code of Conduct, including harassment, discrimination, intimidation, bullying, or Gender based Violence, the Executive Committee may also notify the relevant Faculty for awareness, even where the Society is not seeking any particular action from the University.
The Executive Committee reserves the right to take immediate action where it considers this necessary to maintain a safe, respectful, and inclusive environment for all members.
If you are being harassed, witness harassment, or have any other concerns, please contact a member of the Executive Committee immediately. Contact information is available through our official channels.

# Conduct Towards Organisers and Executive Members

Executive members, volunteers, and other organisers are responsible for maintaining a safe event environment, managing shared or limited event resources fairly, and giving reasonable directions to participants. Members must treat organisers with the same courtesy and respect expected of any other interaction covered by this Code, and must respect the authority of the organising team to make decisions and give directions during an event.

Members must not be rude, dismissive, confrontational, or hostile towards organisers, including when they are being given a direction they disagree with, asked to wait their turn for shared or limited event resources, or told that a request cannot be accommodated. Raising a genuine concern or asking an organiser to reconsider a decision is welcome and is not, by itself, a breach of this Code. But this must be done politely and through a reasonable tone and manner. Talking back, arguing persistently after a decision has been made, raising your voice, or being sarcastic, condescending, or intimidating towards an organiser is not acceptable and will be treated as a breach of this Code in its own right, separate from whether the underlying concern was valid.

# Executive Wellbeing and Capacity

The Executive Committee has a responsibility to lead the Society well, and to do so sustainably. Executive members should:
- Ask for help with tasks they are struggling with, or do not have the capacity to complete.
- Support other Executive members if they see them struggling.
- Take on only what they are reasonably able to complete within their capacity.
- Maintain an appropriate and healthy balance between university study, work, and Executive commitments, and prioritise their own health and wellbeing above Society matters where the two are in genuine conflict.

# Conflict Resolution Within the Executive Team
Where a conflict arises within the Executive Committee, the matter should be dealt with in accordance with this Code. The President has a responsibility to make fair decisions in the best interest of the Society and may act as a mediator between Executive members. Where a disagreement is serious, or where the QUT Guild's involvement would assist, the President should work with the Guild to help resolve the issue. Where a conflict involves the President directly, another Executive member, or the Guild, should act as mediator instead.

# Joint and Multi Society Events

Some Society events, including hackathons and similar activities, are organised jointly with other QUT clubs or societies. Where an event is jointly organised:
- This Code applies to Society members and to the event as a whole, alongside the Code of Conduct of any co organising club, to the extent that the other club's Code also applies.
- Decisions about a participant's conduct at the event, including any interim removal or suspension, will generally be made jointly by the organising committees involved, having regard to the standards set out in each Code.
- Where a matter may need to be reported to the University, the organising committees will generally coordinate to provide the University with a single, consistent account of the incident and the reasons for any decision made, rather than separate or inconsistent reports.
- Evidence and records relating to an incident at a jointly organised event should, where practicable, be retained jointly or shared between the organising committees on a need to know basis, in accordance with the confidentiality provisions below.

# Evidence and Record Keeping

Where the Executive Committee investigates a concern or makes a decision under this Code, it should keep an appropriate record of the information it relied upon, including the general nature of any reports received, the reasons for the decision made, and any warning or direction given to a member. Records containing personal, financial, payment, or other confidential information should be stored securely and access to them should be limited to those Executive members who have a genuine need to know, rather than being circulated broadly within the Society. Where a matter may be raised again in future, including with the University, this record allows the Society to give an accurate and consistent account of what occurred and why a decision was made, without needing to rely on memory alone.

# Communication with Faculty and Other External Parties

Where the Executive Committee decides to notify the Faculty, the University, or another external party about a conduct matter, it should make clear:
- Whether the Society is seeking any particular action from that party, or whether the communication is provided for awareness and future reference only.
- That any decision made by the Society, such as an interim removal from an event, was an event management decision and not a formal finding of misconduct under a University process.
- That some information relied upon may be sensitive or confidential, and has therefore been retained by the organising committee rather than broadly shared, with an offer to provide further detail through an appropriate confidential channel if required.
Members should be aware that a complaint made in good faith to the University or Faculty about the Society's handling of a matter is not, by itself, a breach of this Code, and members will not be penalised for raising such a complaint through an appropriate channel.

# Alignment with the QUT Student Code of Conduct

This Code is intended to operate alongside, and consistently with, the QUT Student Code of Conduct. In particular, members should be aware that the University's Code:
- Requires students to treat others with respect and courtesy, refrain from intimidating, bullying, harassing, or threatening behaviour, and comply with reasonable directions given by University Officers.
- Recognises that misconduct can arise from a single incident or from a pattern of conduct, and can occur on or off campus, online, or in any context connected with the University, including at club events.
- Treats Gender based Violence, harassment, and discrimination as serious matters that may be managed separately under the University's Gender based Violence Prevention and Response Policy.
- Protects freedom of expression, while making clear that this freedom does not extend to conduct that harasses, vilifies, defames, or intimidates others, or unreasonably limits the rights of others.
Where conduct at a Society event may also constitute misconduct under the University's Code, the Society's decision to manage the immediate event, for example by issuing a warning or removing a participant, is separate from, and does not replace, any investigation or determination the University may make under its own processes.

# Executive Responsibilities

Executive members represent the Society and are expected to uphold a higher standard of conduct.
Executive members are expected to:
- Lead by example.
- Act in the best interests of the Society.
- Treat members, faculty, sponsors, and industry representatives professionally.
- Maintain appropriate confidentiality regarding internal Executive discussions.
- Coordinate external communications through the appropriate Executive channels where required.
- Exercise sound judgement when representing the Society publicly.
- Continue to respect the confidentiality of information they became aware of through their Executive role, even after they leave the Society or their position.

# Reporting Concerns

Members are encouraged to report behaviour that may breach this Code of Conduct.
Reports will be handled respectfully and, where reasonably practicable, confidentially by the Executive Committee.
The Executive Committee may investigate concerns, gather information from relevant parties, and determine an appropriate outcome based on the available evidence. A member who reports a concern in good faith, or who is involved in a concern being investigated, will not be penalised for having raised or been involved in that report.
